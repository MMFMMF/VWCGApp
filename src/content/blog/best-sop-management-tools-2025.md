You've decided to document your processes. Good. Now you need somewhere to put them — and a shared Google Drive folder isn't going to cut it past about 20 SOPs.

SOP management tools solve three problems that documents and spreadsheets can't: **discoverability** (can your team find the right SOP when they need it?), **version control** (is this the current version?), and **accountability** (has everyone read and acknowledged the update?).

Here's an honest breakdown of the best options for small and mid-size businesses, along with who each tool is actually built for.

## What to Look For in an SOP Tool

Before comparing tools, know what matters for *your* situation:

**Must-haves for any business:**
- Easy editing (if it's harder to update than a Google Doc, nobody will maintain it)
- Search functionality (SOPs are useless if people can't find them in 10 seconds)
- Version history (you need to know what changed and when)
- Access controls (not everyone needs to edit everything)

**Important for growing teams (15+ people):**
- Onboarding workflows (assign SOPs to new hires with completion tracking)
- Acknowledgment tracking (confirm everyone has read critical updates)
- Role-based views (show people only the SOPs relevant to their job)

**Nice-to-haves:**
- Mobile access (critical for field teams, less important for desk workers)
- Video embedding (great for visual processes)
- Integration with your existing tools (Slack, Teams, your LMS)
- AI-assisted writing or formatting

## The Top SOP Management Tools

### 1. Trainual

**Best for:** Growing businesses (15-100 employees) that need onboarding + SOP management in one platform.

**What it does well:**
Trainual combines SOP documentation with employee onboarding and training tracking. You build "subjects" (SOPs, policies, role descriptions) and assign them to roles. When someone joins or changes roles, they automatically get the relevant material with completion tracking.

The editor is intuitive — similar to a modern blog editor. You can embed videos, add screenshots, create quizzes, and organize content by department or role. The role-based structure means your marketing team only sees marketing SOPs, not warehouse procedures.

**Where it falls short:**
- Gets expensive as you scale (per-seat pricing adds up)
- More opinionated structure — if your SOPs don't fit neatly into "subjects" and "topics," it can feel constraining
- Limited workflow/approval features for SOP changes

**Pricing:** Starts around $250/month for small teams. Per-seat pricing increases with headcount.

**Best if:** You want one platform for both onboarding and ongoing SOP management, and you're willing to adopt Trainual's organizational structure.

### 2. SweetProcess

**Best for:** Process-focused businesses that want dedicated SOP tooling without the training platform overhead.

**What it does well:**
SweetProcess is purpose-built for procedures and processes. It distinguishes between procedures (step-by-step how-tos), processes (multi-procedure workflows), and policies (rules and guidelines). This taxonomy matters when you're managing hundreds of documents.

Standout features include automatic flowchart generation from your written steps, team assignment with due dates, and a clean interface that gets out of the way. Version history is strong, and you can see exactly who's viewed and acknowledged each SOP.

**Where it falls short:**
- Less polished UI compared to Trainual
- No built-in onboarding workflow (it's SOPs, not a learning platform)
- Limited integrations compared to larger platforms

**Pricing:** Starts around $99/month. Pricing is more straightforward than most competitors.

**Best if:** You're serious about process documentation and want a tool that's built specifically for that — nothing more, nothing less.

### 3. Notion

**Best for:** Teams that want maximum flexibility and already use Notion for other things.

**What it does well:**
Notion isn't an SOP tool — it's a workspace that can be *configured* as an SOP tool. That flexibility is both its greatest strength and weakness. You can build exactly the system you want: databases for SOP metadata, templates for consistent formatting, linked views for role-based access, and nested pages for complex processes.

The editor is excellent — best-in-class for mixing text, media, databases, and embeds. The collaborative editing is smooth. And since many teams already use Notion for projects, wikis, and notes, adding SOPs doesn't require a new tool.

**Where it falls short:**
- No built-in SOP-specific features (acknowledgment tracking, completion tracking, automatic flowcharts)
- You have to build the system yourself — templates exist, but setup and maintenance take effort
- Search works but isn't as targeted as purpose-built SOP tools
- No audit trail for who's read what without manual tracking

**Pricing:** Free tier is generous. Team plan starts at $10/user/month.

**Best if:** You're already in Notion, you have someone willing to build and maintain the SOP system, and you don't need compliance-grade tracking.

### 4. Scribe

**Best for:** Teams that struggle to write SOPs and want to auto-generate them from screen recordings.

**What it does well:**
Scribe records your screen while you complete a process, then automatically generates a step-by-step guide with annotated screenshots. It's remarkably effective for software-based processes — click-by-click documentation that would take 30 minutes to write manually gets generated in real-time.

You can edit the auto-generated steps, add context, and organize guides into collections. The Chrome extension makes capture effortless.

**Where it falls short:**
- Only works for screen-based processes (not physical tasks)
- Auto-generated steps sometimes need significant editing
- Not a full SOP management platform — better as a documentation *creator* than a documentation *manager*
- Limited organization and taxonomy features

**Pricing:** Free tier for basic guides. Pro plans start around $29/user/month.

**Best if:** Your SOPs are primarily software-based and your biggest bottleneck is the time it takes to write documentation.

### 5. Process Street

**Best for:** Teams that need recurring checklist-based workflows with SOP documentation built in.

**What it does well:**
Process Street combines SOP documentation with active workflow execution. You create a template (the SOP), then run instances of that template as checklists. Each run tracks who completed which step and when. Think of it as "SOPs you actively work through" rather than "SOPs you reference."

Conditional logic lets you build branching workflows. Integrations with Zapier and native apps allow automating handoffs. The approval workflow system is strong for regulated processes.

**Where it falls short:**
- The workflow-first approach can feel heavyweight for simple reference SOPs
- UI has a learning curve
- Pricing scales up quickly with features

**Pricing:** Starts around $100/month. Feature-gated tiers.

**Best if:** Your SOPs are active checklists that people work through (onboarding, audits, maintenance procedures) rather than passive reference documents.

## The Real Question: Do You Need a Tool Yet?

Here's the honest answer most software comparison posts won't give you: **if you have fewer than 10 SOPs, you don't need a dedicated tool.**

Google Docs with a shared folder and clear naming convention works fine for early-stage documentation. The ROI on SOP software kicks in when:

- You have 20+ SOPs and people can't find what they need
- You're onboarding more than 5 people per quarter
- Compliance requires audit trails of who's read what
- Your SOPs cross departments and need role-based access

Invest in the *habit* of documenting processes first. The tool is just a container — if you don't have the discipline to create and maintain SOPs, the fanciest platform won't help.

## Our Recommendation

**For most SMBs (15-50 employees):** Start with SweetProcess or Trainual. SweetProcess if you want focused SOP management. Trainual if you also want onboarding built in.

**For budget-conscious teams already in Notion:** Build your SOP system there. It won't have tracking features, but it's free and your team already knows the tool.

**For teams with heavy software processes:** Combine Scribe (for capturing) with Notion or SweetProcess (for managing).

**Before choosing any tool:** Assess where your operational gaps actually are. The biggest SOP-related improvements come from documenting the *right* processes, not from picking the perfect tool. Start with a structured assessment of your operations — understand which processes are costing you the most in errors, training time, or inconsistency — then document those first.
